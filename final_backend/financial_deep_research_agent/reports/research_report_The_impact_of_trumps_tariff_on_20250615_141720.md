## The Impact of Trump's Tariffs on the World Economy in 2025

### Executive Summary

This report examines the projected impact of tariffs, particularly those enacted during the Trump presidency, on the global economy in 2025. Analysis of reports from organizations like the OECD and UN suggests a likely global economic slowdown, partially attributable to increased trade costs and policy uncertainty stemming from protectionist measures such as tariffs. While the overall outlook is negative, some economies may find opportunities to adapt. However, significant uncertainties and risks remain, necessitating continuous monitoring of the global economy.

### Introduction

During Donald Trump's presidency (2017-2021), the United States implemented a series of tariffs on various goods, most notably targeting China. These measures, often framed as a response to unfair trade practices and aimed at protecting domestic industries, have had far-reaching consequences for global trade and economic growth. This report assesses the projected impact of these tariffs on the world economy in 2025, considering factors such as trade disruptions, inflation, regional impacts, and potential mitigating factors.

### Main Findings

**1. Global Economic Slowdown:**

*   The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) projects a slowdown in global growth to 2.9% in 2025, down from 3.3% in 2024, attributing this to "rising trade costs" driven by import levies and policy uncertainty.
*   The UN projects a global growth slowdown to 2.4% in 2025, down from 2.9% in 2024, reflecting heightened trade tensions and policy uncertainty straining supply chains and delaying investments.
*   Some analyses suggest that 2025 could see the weakest global growth since 2008 (excluding the pandemic year), primarily due to persistent US policy disruptions.

**2. Trade and Supply Chain Disruptions:**

*   Global trade growth is expected to slow substantially.
*   Tariffs and policy uncertainty could cause "significant disruptions" to the global supply chain.
*   Rising protectionism is making the world more fragmented, with smaller economies facing high inflation and output volatility.
*   Companies are expected to continue rerouting supply chains.
*   The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) warned of approaching stagnation in regional trade due to rising tariffs.

**3. Inflation:**

*   Increased trade costs from tariffs could drive up inflation.
*   The impact on inflation could be offset by weaker commodity prices.

**4. Regional Impacts:**

*   **United States:** Industrial output in the US is projected to shrink, and US GDP growth is projected to slow to 1.6% in 2025.
*   **China:** China's GDP growth is expected to moderate.
*   **Eurozone:** The Eurozone is expected to face below-potential growth due to trade disruptions, with the OECD forecasting growth at just 1% in 2025.
*   **Canada and Mexico:** These countries, heavily dependent on US trade, are likely to bear the brunt of the impact of US tariffs.
*   **Developing Asia and the Pacific:** Global trade tensions and slowing external demand are dampening growth prospects.
*   **Sub-Saharan Africa:** Real income per capita in Sub-Saharan Africa in 2025 is expected to be only 0.6 percent lower than its level in 2019

**5. Uncertainty and Risks:**

*   Elevated uncertainty is expected to weigh on cyclical growth and dampen potential growth.
*   US trade and fiscal policy remain sources of deep uncertainty for the global economy.
*   Geopolitical tensions and the rise of geoeconomic blocs will continue to redefine supply chain dynamics.
*   The baseline scenario remains dominated by downside risks, and a continuing fragmentation in global trade or prolonged uncertainty could deepen the current slowdown.

**6. Potential Opportunities:**

*   Some analyses suggest that larger economies, particularly Asian giants and the BRICS bloc, may see opportunities in the post-adjustment phase to establish new trade frameworks.
*   "Connector Economies" with advantageous locations and trade agreements across major blocs may benefit.

**7. Mitigating Factors:**

*   A temporary agreement between the United States and China to ease recent tariff hikes could provide some relief.
*   China is set to bolster its economy with fiscal stimulus, which may aid its GDP growth.
*   Anticipated monetary policy easing may help to recalibrate economies.

### Analysis

The projected economic slowdown in 2025 reflects the lasting impact of protectionist trade policies. The uncertainty generated by these policies discourages investment and disrupts established trade relationships.

The fragmentation of global trade poses a risk to smaller economies, which may struggle to adapt to the shifting landscape. However, larger economies with greater resources and more diversified trade relationships may be able to capitalize on new opportunities.

The interconnectedness of the global economy means that trade policies implemented by major players like the United States have far-reaching consequences, impacting countries and regions across the globe. The extent of these impacts may be amplified or mitigated by geopolitical tensions, fiscal policies, and monetary easing.

### Conclusion

The evidence suggests that the tariffs imposed during the Trump administration will continue to exert a drag on global economic growth in 2025. While the magnitude of the impact remains uncertain and may be influenced by various mitigating factors, the overall outlook points to a weaker global economy characterized by trade disruptions, policy uncertainty, and regional disparities. Further research is needed to better quantify the regional and sectoral impacts of these policies and to assess the effectiveness of potential mitigation strategies. Continuous monitoring of the global economy is essential to navigate the challenges and opportunities presented by the evolving trade landscape.
